The Book — Chapter List & Title Candidates

*Working structure. Serious chapters carry the argument; the speculative/silly ones are spark material — keep, mutate, or kill. One measured result (the code-review chapter) is the keel; everything else is rigging.*

Part I — The Line

- **The Crawler's Secret.** Origin story: optimizing web re-observation in 1999 and finding the hard line — pages beyond which no re-observation is *ever* attempted. Water-filling, Koopman, the shadow price. The reader meets the invariant before meeting any institution.
- **The 700-Token Review.** A language model asked to review 8× the code writes the same-size review. The saturating channel, measured. Review-per-line collapsing 10× is not laziness — it's the optimal policy's signature. (The book's owned experimental result lives here.)
- **Zero Is Not Small.** The chapter about the cliff: below the line isn't "skimmed," it's invisible. LGTM as the sound an unread artifact makes. The three-way distinction the whole book turns on: reviewed / reviewed-badly / not-reviewed-at-all.

Part II — Ten Rooms, One Machine

- **The Instrument Panel.** The inversion: human factors, the basic T, the dark cockpit. Seventy years of arranging the world to fit the channel instead of asking the channel to grow. The chapter that hands the reader the design vocabulary.
- **Materiality.** Financial audit as the domain honest enough to *codify* its attention line (the materiality threshold) — and Wirecard as what happens when the independent sensor is replaced by the auditee's own map.
- **In-Family.** Challenger and Columbia: normalization of deviance as paperwork that moves a live signal below the line. The router captured not by malice but by schedule.
- **The Certified Self.** ODA delegation and the 737 MAX; automotive self-certification and the NCAP pedestrian robot with the radar cross-section of a car. A frozen attention line is a published exploit spec. Approval theatre as a stable equilibrium.
- **Boilerplate.** Contracts, omnibus bills, supplementary materials: the regions *everyone knows* are dark, and the payloads that live there (pari passu; riders; the Reinhart–Rogoff spreadsheet nobody ran). Includes the XZ backdoor as the masterpiece of reverse-engineering a reviewer's salience function.
- **Alarm Fatigue.** Medicine's inverted dark cockpit: light everything and you've darkened everything. Checklists as the counter-move — and why the fix was *removing* items from attention, not adding vigilance.

Part III — The Router Arrives

- **The Escape Ledger.** Mining repositories for reviewed-and-approved code that later broke; severity as price; the misalignment figure (where comments cluster vs. where escapes cluster). The learned attention router — and reviewer-specific blind-spot profiles.
- **Who Audits the Dark?** The off-policy trap: a live router stops receiving data from the regions it darkened. The epsilon trickle as the one budget the router must never own. (The thesis leftover that turned out to be the design law.)
- **The Feed Was the Prototype.** The attention economy as the domain that built the router *first* and skipped the review layer entirely: engagement optimization = the steering experiment at planet scale. Not a morality tale — an existence proof, with the moral variable isolated to a single term: whose objective the router serves.
- **The Guardian.** The chief-of-staff agent in front of every professional. Same architecture as the feed, opposite principal — and every pathology from Part II inherited on day one. What a trustworthy guardian minimally requires (independent salience, severity pricing, a principal-owned audit trickle).

Part IV — Shaping Without Sin

- **Comedy Is a Compiler.** The tight loop: small clubs as debug seeds, the laugh as an involuntary, millisecond-resolved trace, the special as held-out eval. Craft traditions as accumulated attention-routing skill libraries (writing rules, "kill your darlings" — rules about the loop itself).
- **Teaching Is Attention Shaping.** The benevolent pole: a curriculum is a router; a good teacher spends their entire skill budget deciding what the student does *not* see yet. Scaffolding, desirable difficulties, and why "just show everything" is the alarm-fatigue of pedagogy. (Bridge to the education work: what is the irreducibly human core of a co-creative learning community, if the routing can be learned?)
- **Writing for the Line.** Review-optimized presentation: dark-cockpit documents, claims sorted by verifiability, the permutation result (same content, different order, different catch rate). What "well-written" means when the reader is a saturating channel — Strunk & White as channel-matching, discovered generationally.

Speculative / silly chapters (spark material — some of these want to be real)

- **S1. The Attention Heist.** A fully worked fictional caper: a team plans and executes the perfect below-the-line payload against a modern review pipeline (told like Ocean's Eleven; every move cites a real mechanism from Parts I–II). Possibly the most memorable chapter in the book if it lands; possibly irresponsible. Decide late.
- **S2. A Field Guide to Dark Regions.** Illustrated bestiary, two pages per creature: the Boilerplate Clause, the Shop Drawing, the Supplementary Material, the Test Fixture, the Vendored Dependency, the Migration Script, the Friday 4:55pm CL. Deadpan taxonomy voice.
- **S3. The Peacock's Diff.** Attention *display* in the animal kingdom — signals evolved to capture a mate's or predator's scarce evaluation budget; honest-signaling theory (Zahavi's handicap) as nature's answer to review theatre. Costly signals as the original execution-verified catch.
- **S4. The Museum of Unread Things.** A walking tour: EULAs, safety cards, org-wide emails, the middle slides of every deck, page 2 of search results, the second paragraph of this chapter (prove it). Interactive gimmick: the chapter contains one deliberate absurdity and tells you so up front; the endnotes reveal where. Measures the reader's own line.
- **S5. Attention Karma.** Thought experiment: a market where review attention is a priced, tradeable resource — CLs post bounties, reviewers stake reputation, escapes claw back the stake. Work out where it collapses (Goodhart, collusion, the poor going unreviewed) and what the collapse teaches about the non-market versions we already run.
- **S6. The Sommelier Protocol.** Expertise as a trained salience function: what a chess master, a radiologist, and a senior reviewer literally do not see (inattentional blindness as the *cost* of a good router). The chapter where the book admits its hero has a shadow.
- **S7. Do LLMs Dream of Unread Tokens?** The playful-technical one: attention sinks, lost-in-the-middle, the model's own positional darkness — the reviewer we built inherits a line we didn't design. Written as a dialogue with a model. (Risk: cute. Mitigation: data.)

Five title candidates

- **Below the Line** — *The Hidden Economics of Review, from Code to Cockpits to Congress*

- **The Unread World** — *Scarce Attention, Captured Reviewers, and the Machines That Route What We See*
- **Zero Is Not Small** — *A Theory of What Never Gets Looked At*
